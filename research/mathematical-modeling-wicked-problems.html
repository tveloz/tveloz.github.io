What are Wicked Problems?

Wicked problems are complex societal challenges that resist traditional solution approaches. Coined by Rittel and Webber in 1973, these problems are characterized by their lack of definitive formulation, having no stopping rule, and possessing solutions that are neither true nor false, but rather better or worse.

Dr. Veloz applies systemic modeling frameworks, particularly Chemical Organization Theory and complex systems approaches, to tackle problems where reductionist paradigms fail due to the systemic nature of the phenomena. These include climate change, poverty, education inequality, and public health crises.

Characteristics of Wicked Problems

  • No definitive formulation: Problem definition evolves
  • No stopping rule: Solutions are never final
  • Better/worse solutions: Not true/false
  • Unique problems: Each is essentially novel
